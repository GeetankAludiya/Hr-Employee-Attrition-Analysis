# HR Employee Attrition Analysis 📊

## 📌 Project Overview

This project analyzes employee attrition using HR workforce data to identify patterns and factors associated with employee turnover.

The analysis combines **Excel data cleaning, calculated categories, pivot tables, KPI analysis, and an interactive-style dashboard** to provide a clear view of attrition across different employee characteristics.

The objective is to help HR teams understand **where attrition is highest, which employee groups are most affected, and which areas may require retention-focused strategies.**

---

## 🎯 Business Problem

Employee attrition can increase recruitment costs, reduce productivity, and create workforce instability.

This analysis aims to answer questions such as:

* What is the overall employee attrition rate?
* Which age groups have the highest attrition?
* Which departments and job roles experience greater turnover?
* Does job satisfaction relate to attrition?
* How does tenure affect employee turnover?
* Is attrition higher among lower-income employees?
* Does overtime or business travel appear to be associated with higher attrition?

## 🔍 Key Insights

### 1. Younger employees have significantly higher attrition

Employees aged **18–25** have an attrition rate of approximately **35.8%**, the highest among all age groups.

The attrition rate for employees aged **36–45** is approximately **9.2%**.

This suggests that early-career employees may require stronger onboarding, career development, mentoring, and retention programs.

### 2. Sales has the highest departmental attrition rate

The **Sales department** has an attrition rate of approximately **20.6%**, compared with:

* Human Resources: **19.0%**
* Research & Development: **13.8%**

Sales therefore represents an important area for further investigation.

### 3. Sales Representatives show the highest job-role attrition

Among job roles, **Sales Representatives** have the highest attrition rate at approximately **39.8%**.

Other relatively high-attrition roles include:

* Laboratory Technician: **23.9%**
* Human Resources: **23.1%**
* Sales Executive: **17.5%**
* Research Scientist: **16.1%**

In contrast, Research Directors have an attrition rate of approximately **2.5%**.

### 4. Early-tenure employees are at higher risk

Employees with **0–1 years at the company** have an attrition rate of approximately **34.9%**.

This falls to:

* 15.5% for 2–5 years
* 12.3% for 6–10 years
* 6.7% for 11–20 years

The first year appears to be a particularly important retention period.

### 5. Lower monthly income groups show higher attrition

Employees earning **$1,000–$2,500 per month** have an attrition rate of approximately **34.4%**.

The $2,500–$4,000 group has an attrition rate of approximately **18.9%**, while several higher-income groups show substantially lower attrition.

This suggests that compensation may be one factor worth considering alongside other employee-experience variables.

### 6. Overtime is strongly associated with attrition

Employees working overtime have an attrition rate of approximately **30.5%**, compared with approximately **10.4%** for employees who do not work overtime.

This is one of the clearest differences observed in the analysis and may indicate a relationship between workload, work-life balance, and employee turnover.

### 7. Frequent business travel is associated with higher attrition

Employees who travel frequently have an attrition rate of approximately **24.9%**.

This compares with:

* Travel Rarely: **15.0%**
* Non-Travel: **8.0%**

Frequent travel may therefore be another factor worth investigating in retention strategies.

### 8. Job satisfaction is associated with attrition

Employees in the **Low Job Satisfaction** category have an attrition rate of approximately **22.8%**, compared with approximately **11.3%** among employees with **Very High Job Satisfaction**.

This indicates that employee experience and satisfaction may play an important role in retention.

## 💡 Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis, organizations could consider:

1. **Strengthening first-year employee retention programs**

   * Improve onboarding
   * Introduce mentoring
   * Conduct early employee check-ins

2. **Reviewing overtime and workload**

   * Monitor excessive overtime
   * Improve workload distribution
   * Encourage healthier work-life balance

3. **Focusing on high-risk job roles**

   * Investigate Sales Representative turnover
   * Review role-specific workload and compensation
   * Identify career progression barriers

4. **Improving employee satisfaction**

   * Conduct regular engagement surveys
   * Identify causes of low satisfaction
   * Develop targeted engagement initiatives

5. **Reviewing compensation for lower-income groups**

   * Benchmark salaries against market rates
   * Evaluate incentive structures
   * Improve career progression opportunities

6. **Supporting frequent travelers**

   * Review travel workload
   * Improve travel policies and support
   * Monitor employee fatigue and satisfaction

---

## ⚠️ Important Note

The analysis identifies **associations and patterns**, not causal relationships.

For example, the higher attrition rate among employees working overtime does not by itself prove that overtime causes employees to leave. Further statistical analysis and employee-level investigation would be required to establish causality.
